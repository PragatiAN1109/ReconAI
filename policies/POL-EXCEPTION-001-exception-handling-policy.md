# Exception Handling Policy

> Synthetic demonstration document. Not a real exception handling policy.

## Exception Lifecycle

A reconciliation exception is opened when a discrepancy is detected and progresses
through investigation and review before it can be resolved. An exception is never
closed automatically on the basis of a plausible explanation.

## Human Authorisation

An automated investigation may gather evidence and propose an explanation. It may not
resolve an exception, adjust a financial record, or initiate any movement of money. A
proposed explanation is advisory until an authorised reviewer accepts it.

## Escalation on Insufficient Evidence

Where the available evidence does not support a conclusion, escalation is the correct
outcome. Escalation is a successful result, not a failure: an unsupported explanation
recorded as fact is more damaging than an acknowledged gap.

Escalate where no fee rule, policy or historical pattern accounts for a difference;
where evidence supports more than one explanation and cannot distinguish between them;
where the amounts involved exceed the materiality threshold for automated handling; or
where the same discrepancy recurs after a previous resolution.

## Duplicate Settlement Handling

A suspected duplicate settlement must not be reversed until the cause is established.
Reversing a settlement that was in fact a correction creates a second, larger
discrepancy.

## Recurrence

An exception that recurs after resolution indicates the underlying cause was not
addressed. Recurring exceptions of the same type for the same merchant and processor
should be reviewed together rather than individually.
